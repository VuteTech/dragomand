# About

Dragomand is written and maintained by
[Blagovest Petrov](https://petrovs.info). The idea was simple:
Mozilla's translation models are good, free and run on an
ordinary laptop, so every application on the Linux desktop should be
able to use them, offline, without each one shipping its own copy.
Development is sponsored by [Vute Tech Ltd.](https://vute.tech)

The same author makes the clients around the daemon:
[Krakoman](krakoman.md), the [KDE System Settings module](system-settings.md),
the [Plasma widget](plasma-widget.md), the
[KTextEditor plugin](ktexteditor.md), the
[LibreOffice extension](libreoffice.md) and the
[Qt client library](libdragoman-qt.md). Everything is free software
under the GPL, version 3 or later, and developed in the open on
[GitHub](https://github.com/VuteTech). Bug reports, translations and
patches are welcome there.

## Thanks

Dragomand stands on the work of many people.

- **Mozilla** trains and publishes the translation models, and keeps
  releasing new languages and better versions of the old ones. They are
  the models behind
  [Firefox Translations](https://www.mozilla.org/firefox/features/translate/),
  and Mozilla distributes them under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, so
  that other projects such as this one can use them. The models and the
  engine code come from Mozilla's
  [translations](https://github.com/mozilla/translations) project.
- **[The Bergamot project](https://browser.mt/)**, in which the
  University of Edinburgh, Charles University, the University of
  Sheffield, the University of Tartu and Mozilla made fast machine
  translation run locally on ordinary computers, with funding from the
  European Union's Horizon 2020 programme. Its bergamot-translator,
  which Mozilla now maintains, is the engine Dragomand runs.
- **[Marian](https://marian-nmt.github.io/)**, the neural machine
  translation framework inside that engine, together with intgemm,
  ruy, SentencePiece and the other libraries it builds on.
- **The KDE community**, for KDE Frameworks, Kirigami and Plasma, which
  the graphical clients are built with.

## The name

**Dragomand** is *dragoman* + *d*. The *d* is the old Unix suffix for
a daemon, as in `sshd` or `systemd`, and the command-line client keeps
the root: `dragomanctl`. The word *dragoman* has more than one meaning.

A [dragoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoman) was an interpreter,
translator and official guide. Dragomans worked between Turkish-,
Arabic- and Persian-speaking lands and the European embassies,
consulates and trading posts of the Middle East. The word is very old:
it goes back to Akkadian *targumannu*, then Aramaic *targemana*, Arabic
*tarjumān* and Turkish *tercüman*. It reached Europe through Greek and
Italian and became the English *dragoman*. Bulgarian has the same word,
*драгоман*.

In the Ottoman Empire the office carried real power. The Grand
Dragoman of the Sublime Porte, often a Phanariote Greek such as
Panagiotis Nikousios or Alexander Mavrocordatos, stood between the
Sultan's government and the foreign powers. Several of them later
became rulers of the Danubian Principalities. Western dragomans carried
texts the other way: Antoine Galland, who translated the *Thousand and
One Nights* into French, started out as a dragoman.

Dragoman is also a
[small town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoman,_Bulgaria)
(Драгоман) in Sofia Province, western Bulgaria, close to the
Serbian border and on the road and railway from Sofia to Belgrade. It
is the seat of Dragoman Municipality. Nearby is the Dragoman Marsh, a
karst wetland. Bulgaria's Antarctic place names include the Dragoman
Glacier on Smith Island, named after the town.
